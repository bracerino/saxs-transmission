How it works

The linear attenuation coefficient of the sample is built up from its phases:

µ = (1 − P) · Σi φi ρi Σj wij (µ/ρ)j(E)

φi is the volume fraction of phase i in the solid (converted from wt % if needed), ρi its density, wij the mass fraction of element j in it, and P the porosity. Transmission follows Beer–Lambert, T = exp(−µt), and the thickness for a target transmission is t = −ln(T)/µ.

Optimal SAXS thickness. The scattered intensity scales as t·e−µt, which peaks at t = 1/µ, i.e. T ≈ 37 %.

Data. Total cross sections (photoabsorption + coherent + incoherent scattering) from Elam, Ravel & Sieber, Radiat. Phys. Chem. 63 (2002) 121, interpolated with log-log cubic splines.

This is an estimate. Real densities, residual solvent, and surface roughness all shift the answer, so measure the transmission on the instrument.
